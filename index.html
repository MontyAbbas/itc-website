Areas of expertise

Where the analysis starts

Each matter turns on what the roadway, the signal, and the driver were doing at a specific moment. These are the technical questions ITC is retained to answer.

Traffic signal timing and controller logic

Reconstruction of timing plans and signal sequencing from controller event logs; interpretation of NEMA and Type 170 controller behavior; coordination, preemption, and transition events.

ATSPM high-resolution data · NEMA / 170 · SPaT / MAP

Intersection and roadway safety, crash causation

Contributing-factor analysis for crashes at signalized intersections, arterials, interchanges, and transportation facilities, applying the Highway Safety Manual and MUTCD.

HSM · MUTCD · AASHTO

Dilemma-zone protection and clearance intervals

Yellow-change and all-red interval adequacy, advance detection, and dilemma-zone systems. Dr. Abbas developed, or was part of the teams that evaluated, D-CS, PIA, and AWEGS for TxDOT and FHWA.

D-CS · PIA · AWEGS · advance warning flashers

Driver behavior and human factors

Driver decision-making at the onset of yellow, perception-reaction, and learning effects, drawing on driving-simulator studies, in-vehicle video, and cognitive modeling.

Driving simulator · naturalistic video · agent-based models

Roadway design and access management

Design-defect and standard-of-care review for arterials, interchanges, terminals, and access points, including sight distance, signing, and geometric conformance.

AASHTO Green Book · MUTCD · agency design standards

Work-zone operations and safety

Temporary traffic control, lane-closure scheduling, and work-zone safety, including the interaction between maintenance decisions and traffic operations.

MUTCD Part 6 · simulation-optimization

Microsimulation and digital-twin reconstruction

Calibrated microsimulation and hardware-in-the-loop testing to reproduce operating conditions, test alternative timings, and quantify what a design or control change would have done.

VISSIM · SUMO · Aimsun · AnyLogic · CORSIM · Synchro · HIL / CIL

Traffic-technology patents and algorithms

Technical analysis of accused traffic-control technologies and prior art, with deposition and trial testimony in federal patent litigation.

Radar, video, and loop detection · adaptive control · V2X

Travel-demand and toll-revenue forecasts

Independent review of travel-demand models and econometric or time-series traffic forecasts submitted in rate proceedings and public-sector decisions.

Demand modeling · time-series forecasting

Transportation program and contract review

Program-management and engineering-services contract review: industry custom for compensation, duplicative services, and quality-assurance practice in design and construction.

QA / QC practice · industry custom

Methodology

Built to withstand Daubert

Opinions rest on methods that are testable, peer-reviewed, standards-based, and reproducible from the underlying data, satisfying the reliability factors of Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals and Federal Rule of Evidence 702.

Testability

Signal-control algorithms developed by Dr. Abbas were tested in hardware-in-the-loop and cabinet-in-the-loop simulation before deployment, then validated in field installations under FHWA, VCTIR, and TxDOT projects.

Peer review

Methods are documented in Transportation Research Record, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, the ASCE Journal of Transportation Engineering, and in FHWA and TxDOT final reports.

Known error rate

Field and simulation evaluations produced quantified performance metrics with documented calibration and validation, including confidence intervals on reductions in dilemma-zone trapping, delay, and stops.

Standards

Analyses conform to the MUTCD, Highway Capacity Manual, Highway Safety Manual, AASHTO design guidance, and FHWA technical guidance where applicable.

General acceptance

The methods are those used in practice by state DOTs, FHWA, and the transportation-engineering research community, as reflected in agency guidance, NCHRP reports, and TRB committee work.

Reproducibility

Workflows for event-log decoding, timing-plan reconstruction, microsimulation calibration, and contributing-factor analysis are documented so another engineer can reproduce them from the same data.

Engagement

How an engagement proceeds

Engagements are contracted and billed through Intelligent Transportation Concepts, LLC. The fee schedule and engagement agreement are provided to counsel on request.

  1. Conflict check

    Counsel provides the caption, parties, and related entities. No substantive information is exchanged until the check clears.

  2. Engagement agreement and retainer

    Scope, fee schedule, and confidentiality terms are set in a signed agreement. A retainer is applied against fees as they accrue.

  3. Review and analysis

    Document, data, and site review; controller event-log decoding; simulation or field work as the matter requires.

  4. Report or declaration

    A Rule 26-compliant report or declaration stating opinions, bases, and the materials considered.

  5. Deposition and trial

    Preparation with counsel and testimony at deposition, hearing, or trial. Reserved dates are held exclusively for the retaining party.

Hourly billing in quarter-hour increments. Fees are never contingent on the outcome of a matter or on the opinions rendered.
The same rates apply to plaintiffs, defendants, petitioners, respondents, and government or regulatory bodies.
Monthly itemized statements showing time, tasks, expenses, and the remaining retainer balance. Unused retainer is refunded.
Non-public information about a matter, including the fact and terms of the engagement, is kept confidential. Work in support of litigation is prepared as attorney work product.
